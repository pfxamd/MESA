## Shared editorial and culinary rules

- Each recipe must have a clearly stated base yield, quantities, sensible order of work, estimated preparation/cook times, equipment, cooking cues, allergen notes, and a fully specified method.
- Pantry matching uses the normalized IDs in square brackets, never substring matches on display names. Salt, water and pepper are pantry basics, but a recipe still lists them; the matching UI should explicitly disclose any staples assumed. Oil, garlic, lemon, butter and spice blends are not invisible free ingredients.
- Ingredient IDs denote interchangeable culinary *materials* only where reasonable: [canned-white-beans], [canned-chickpeas], [fresh-tomatoes], [garlic], [olive-oil], [orange], etc. Different ingredients such as canned tomatoes and fresh tomatoes are distinct.
- Bracketed ingredient IDs are editorial references for the later data model, not visible UI copy. Optional ingredients must be marked optional rather than counted among mandatory match ingredients.
- Dietary categories must be validated against actual packaging, especially bread, mustard, harissa, dairy, tahini and spice blends. Never assume gluten-free or allergen-free from an informal name.
- For serving-size scaling, scale ingredient quantities but do **not** automatically change oven temperature, pan size, temperature safety limits or fixed cook times. Eggs, whole chicken and other indivisible units require explicit sensible rounding/cookware notes.
- Time labels below are estimates, not kitchen measurements. Cooking times and yields should be checked through physical trial.
- Refrigerate perishable cooked food promptly. USDA FSIS guidance: generally within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 32°C/90°F. Keep raw poultry separate from ready-to-eat foods, wash tools and hands after handling, and use a probe thermometer (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/steps-keep-food-safe).
- For R08, poultry must reach **74°C / 165°F** in the thickest breast and innermost thigh and wing without touching bone (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/doneness-versus-safety).
- Recipe steps and ingredient counts should also drive the later photo-brief validation. Do not silently add a garnish simply because an image generator created one.
- Ingredient weights are approximate purchase or drained weights as noted. Volume measurements assume standard metric mL.

## R01 — Charred Tomato Toast
**Slug:** charred-tomato-toast · **Section:** Small Plates · **Serves:** 2 · **Prep:** ~10 min · **Cook:** ~10 min · **Equipment:** frying pan, toaster/grill, mixing bowl.
**Deck:** Hot tomatoes, crisp bread, olive oil and leaves that taste like summer.
**Imagery:** Finished 07; process 19.

### Ingredients
- [fresh-tomatoes] 350 g cherry or small ripe tomatoes
- [sourdough-bread] 2 large thick slices sourdough or country bread (about 160 g)
- [olive-oil] 30 mL extra-virgin olive oil, divided
- [garlic] 1 small clove, halved
- [fresh-basil] 8 g fresh basil leaves, torn
- [fine-salt] 2 g fine salt, or to taste
- [black-pepper] A few grinds

### Method
1. Wash and thoroughly dry the tomatoes. Preheat a heavy frying pan over medium-high heat. Season the tomatoes with half of the olive oil and a small pinch of salt.
2. Add tomatoes in a single layer and cook 5–7 minutes, shaking occasionally, until some skins split and char in spots while the tomatoes stay juicy. Lower heat if the oil smokes.
3. Meanwhile toast the bread on both sides until sturdy and golden; rub each warm slice lightly with the cut garlic.
4. Top the bread with hot tomatoes and any pan juices. Drizzle with remaining olive oil; finish with basil, pepper and the remaining salt.
5. Serve immediately while the bread is crisp; for photography use exactly two large slices and clearly blistered red tomatoes.

**Allergens & checks:** Wheat/gluten in standard sourdough; check specific bread. Use a suitable pan and avoid wet tomatoes spattering into hot oil. **Pantry core:** tomatoes, bread, olive oil, garlic, basil. Salt and pepper disclosed as staples.

## R02 — Lemon & Herb Butter Beans
**Slug:** lemon-herb-butter-beans · **Section:** Small Plates · **Serves:** 2 · **Prep:** ~10 min · **Cook:** ~5 min · **Equipment:** saucepan, colander, bowl, whisk.
**Deck:** Soft white beans lifted by lemon zest, good oil and a fistful of herbs.
**Imagery:** Finished 08; process 20.

### Ingredients
- [canned-white-beans] 480 g canned large white/butter beans, drained and rinsed
- [lemon] 1 medium lemon: about 30 mL juice and 1 tsp finely grated zest
- [olive-oil] 30 mL extra-virgin olive oil
- [flat-leaf-parsley] 20 g flat-leaf parsley, roughly chopped
- [garlic] 1 small clove, finely grated
- [fine-salt] 2 g, adjust to taste
- [black-pepper] A few grinds
- [water] 30 mL warm water, as needed

### Method
1. Drain and rinse the canned beans. Warm them gently with 30 mL water in a saucepan for 3–5 minutes; do not boil vigorously or crush them.
2. Whisk lemon juice, zest, oil, grated garlic and a pinch of salt in a bowl.
3. Drain off excess warming water; fold the warm beans through the lemon dressing.
4. Add parsley, pepper and remaining salt to taste. If the dish looks dry, add a spoonful of warm water rather than too much extra oil.
5. Serve in one shallow ivory bowl, beans visibly whole, fresh parsley bright on top.

**Allergens & checks:** Usually no major named allergens from listed ingredients, but check canned bean packaging and cross-contact. **Pantry core:** white beans, lemon, oil, parsley, garlic.

## R03 — Crispy Potato Salad
**Slug:** crispy-potato-salad · **Section:** Small Plates · **Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** ~15 min · **Cook:** ~45–50 min · **Equipment:** saucepan, tray, oven, bowl.
**Deck:** Rough golden edges with cool mustard-yogurt dressing and dill.
**Imagery:** Finished 09; process 21.

### Ingredients
- [baby-potatoes] 800 g baby potatoes, washed
- [olive-oil] 35 mL olive oil
- [plain-yogurt] 180 g thick plain yogurt
- [dijon-mustard] 15 g Dijon mustard
- [lemon] 15 mL fresh lemon juice
- [fresh-dill] 15 g dill, finely chopped
- [fine-salt] 5 g, divided, adjust to taste
- [black-pepper] A few grinds

### Method
1. Heat oven to **220°C** conventional (200°C fan). Simmer potatoes in salted water for roughly 12–15 minutes, until just fork-tender; drain and steam-dry 5 minutes.
2. Spread on a lightly oiled metal tray. Using the bottom of a sturdy glass or small plate, gently flatten each potato to roughly 1.5 cm, keeping pieces connected. (This is image 21.)
3. Drizzle with olive oil and some salt; roast 30–35 minutes, turning the tray once, until edges are crisp and golden.
4. Mix yogurt, Dijon mustard, lemon juice and chopped dill. Season to taste.
5. Spread dressing under the potatoes or fold very gently just before serving so they retain their crunchy edges. Finish with pepper and remaining dill.

**Allergens & checks:** Milk in yogurt; verify Dijon mustard ingredients and any relevant mustard-allergen requirements in target market. Tray and steam are hot. **Pantry core:** potatoes, olive oil, yogurt, mustard, lemon, dill.

## R04 — Carrot, Orange & Fennel Salad
**Slug:** carrot-orange-fennel-salad · **Section:** Small Plates · **Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** ~20 min · **Cook:** none · **Equipment:** vegetable peeler or mandoline with guard, knife, bowl.
**Deck:** Carrot ribbons and sweet orange cut through with crisp fennel.
**Imagery:** Finished 10; process 22.

### Ingredients
- [carrots] 300 g carrots, peeled
- [fennel] 1 medium fennel bulb (about 250 g trimmed)
- [orange] 2 oranges (about 300 g prepared segments)
- [olive-oil] 25 mL extra-virgin olive oil
- [lemon] 15 mL lemon juice
- [flat-leaf-parsley] 10 g parsley leaves
- [fine-salt] 3 g, or to taste
- [black-pepper] A few grinds

### Method
1. Peel carrots into long ribbons; thinly shave the trimmed fennel with a peeler or a guarded mandoline.
2. Remove orange peel and white pith. Cut segments from between the membranes over a bowl to catch juice.
3. Whisk olive oil and lemon juice with about 15 mL of captured orange juice, salt and pepper.
4. Gently toss carrot, fennel and orange segments with dressing.
5. Build loose layers on a wide flat matte plate; finish with parsley. Serve cool, not ice-cold.

**Allergens & checks:** No major named allergens intentionally included, but check cross-contact. Take care using mandoline/peeler. **Pantry core:** carrots, fennel, orange, olive oil, lemon, parsley.

## R05 — Roasted Pepper & Tomato Pasta
**Slug:** roasted-pepper-tomato-pasta · **Section:** The Main Table · **Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** ~15 min · **Cook:** ~35 min · **Equipment:** oven, tray, blender or potato masher, pan, pasta pot.
**Deck:** A sweet roasted red sauce clinging to ribbons of pasta.
**Imagery:** Finished 11; process 23.

### Ingredients
- [pasta] 320 g dry ribbon pasta (tagliatelle or similar)
- [red-bell-peppers] 2 large red bell peppers (about 350 g prepared)
- [fresh-tomatoes] 500 g ripe tomatoes, halved
- [garlic] 3 cloves, unpeeled for roasting
- [olive-oil] 40 mL olive oil, divided
- [fresh-basil] 15 g basil leaves
- [fine-salt] 5 g, plus extra to season pasta water
- [black-pepper] To taste
- [water] About 120 mL reserved pasta water, use as needed

### Method
1. Heat oven to **210°C** conventional (190°C fan). Slice and deseed peppers, cut tomatoes and place with unpeeled garlic on a tray. Toss with 25 mL oil and a little salt.
2. Roast 25–30 minutes until edges blister and tomatoes collapse. Let cool briefly; squeeze garlic from skins. (The tray before blending is image 23.)
3. Blend roasted vegetables to a thick sauce, or crush for a coarser texture. Transfer to a large pan with remaining oil and simmer 3–5 minutes.
4. Meanwhile cook pasta in well-salted boiling water to package directions until just al dente; reserve pasta water before draining.
5. Toss pasta through warm sauce with small splashes of reserved water until glossy. Finish with torn basil and pepper; serve in a distinctly cobalt-blue dish.

**Allergens & checks:** Wheat/gluten in standard pasta; adapt pasta type deliberately if needed. Handle hot blender contents carefully and vent safely. **Pantry core:** pasta, peppers, tomatoes, garlic, olive oil, basil.

## R06 — Harissa Chickpea Skillet
**Slug:** harissa-chickpea-skillet · **Section:** The Main Table · **Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** ~10 min · **Cook:** ~20 min · **Equipment:** deep skillet, knife, spoon.
**Deck:** Chickpeas and greens simmered in a spirited tomato-and-harissa pan.
**Imagery:** Finished 12; process 24.

### Ingredients
- [canned-chickpeas] 480 g canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- [canned-tomatoes] 400 g chopped canned tomatoes
- [harissa-paste] 25 g harissa paste, or less to taste
- [spinach] 120 g baby spinach
- [onion] 1 medium onion, about 140 g, diced
- [garlic] 2 cloves, minced
- [olive-oil] 25 mL olive oil
- [water] 100 mL water, more only if needed
- [fine-salt] 3 g initially, adjust for salty harissa
- [black-pepper] To taste

### Method
1. Heat oil in a deep skillet over medium heat and soften onion for 5 minutes.
2. Add garlic and harissa; stir for 30–60 seconds without burning the paste.
3. Stir in chopped tomatoes, drained chickpeas and water. Simmer gently 10–12 minutes until sauce coats the beans.
4. Fold in the spinach in batches until just wilted, about 2 minutes. Add salt carefully after tasting the harissa.
5. Bring the skillet directly to the table on a heat-safe trivet; visible whole chickpeas, orange-red sauce and dark green spinach are essential.

**Allergens & checks:** Harissa ingredients vary; check packaging and heat level. Skillet handle may be hot. **Pantry core:** chickpeas, canned tomatoes, harissa, spinach, onion, garlic, olive oil.

## R07 — Crispy Aubergine & Herbed Yogurt
**Slug:** crispy-aubergine-herbed-yogurt · **Section:** The Main Table · **Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** ~15 min · **Cook:** ~30–35 min · **Equipment:** ovenproof frying pan or baking tray, bowl.
**Deck:** Deeply browned aubergine on cool thick yogurt with herbs and toasted seeds.
**Imagery:** Finished 13; process 25.

### Ingredients
- [aubergine] 2 medium aubergines (about 650 g total)
- [plain-yogurt] 250 g thick plain yogurt
- [olive-oil] 45 mL olive oil, divided
- [flat-leaf-parsley] 15 g parsley, finely chopped
- [lemon] 15 mL lemon juice
- [sesame-seeds] 15 g sesame seeds, toasted
- [garlic] 1 small clove, finely grated
- [fine-salt] 4 g, adjust to taste
- [black-pepper] To taste

### Method
1. Heat oven to **200°C** conventional (180°C fan). Halve aubergines lengthwise and score the flesh with shallow crosshatches without cutting through the skin. Season lightly.
2. Heat 30 mL oil in an ovenproof pan and place aubergine halves cut-side down. Sear for 5–7 minutes until well browned; photograph this stage for image 25.
3. Transfer the ovenproof pan or aubergines on a tray to the oven. Roast 20–25 minutes, until completely tender inside; large pieces may need longer.
4. Stir yogurt with lemon, grated garlic, parsley, remaining oil, salt and pepper.
5. Swipe yogurt onto a plate, set aubergines over it, sprinkle with toasted sesame. Show honest browned flesh, not deep-fried breadcrumb coating.

**Allergens & checks:** Milk and sesame. Check pan is ovenproof; its handle will be extremely hot. **Pantry core:** aubergine, yogurt, olive oil, parsley, lemon, sesame, garlic.

## R08 — Golden Roast Chicken & Lemon
**Slug:** golden-roast-chicken-lemon · **Section:** The Main Table · **Serves:** 4–6 · **Prep:** ~15 min · **Cook:** ~75–100 min (temperature governs) · **Rest:** ~15 min · **Equipment:** roasting tray, thermometer, knife.
**Deck:** A golden centrepiece with lemon, garlic and thyme.
**Imagery:** Finished 14; process 26.

### Ingredients
- [whole-chicken] 1 whole unstuffed chicken (approximately 1.5–1.8 kg, fully thawed)
- [lemon] 2 lemons, quartered
- [garlic] 1 whole garlic head, halved across the cloves
- [fresh-thyme] 15 g thyme sprigs
- [olive-oil] 35 mL olive oil
- [fine-salt] About 9 g salt (adjust for bird size)
- [black-pepper] 2 g black pepper
- [water] 100 mL water for roasting tray, if needed to prevent scorching

### Method
1. Heat oven to **200°C** conventional (180°C fan). Keep the chicken refrigerated until preparation. Do not rinse raw chicken. Use clean hands, tools, and a dedicated board; prevent contact with ready-to-eat ingredients.
2. Pat chicken dry with paper towel. Rub outside with oil, salt and pepper; arrange breast up in a roasting tray with lemon quarters, halved garlic and thyme **around** the bird, not stuffed into the cavity. Photograph this raw preparation with safe tool handling as image 26.
3. Roast approximately 75–100 minutes for the stated weight, turning the tray if necessary. Shield overly dark skin with foil; add a little water only if juices threaten to burn. This time is a guide, not a doneness test.
4. Use a clean food thermometer to confirm at least **74°C / 165°F in the thickest breast and the innermost thigh and wing**, avoiding bone. If any reading is lower, continue roasting and recheck. Do not rely on skin colour or clear juices to prove safety.
5. Rest on a clean tray for about 15 minutes before carving. Present the whole browned chicken with cooked lemon and thyme, never raw garnish touching cooked meat.

**Safety:** Raw poultry cross-contamination; strict thermometer rule. USDA FSIS: https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/doneness-versus-safety. **Allergens:** No major named allergens intentionally added; check any purchased ingredient seasoning. **Pantry core:** whole chicken, lemon, garlic, thyme, olive oil.

## R09 — Tomato & Saffron Rice
**Slug:** tomato-saffron-rice · **Section:** The Main Table · **Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** ~10 min · **Cook:** ~30 min · **Rest:** ~5 min · **Equipment:** lidded pot, oven tray or skillet.
**Deck:** Golden grains, soft roasted tomatoes and fresh green herbs.
**Imagery:** Finished 15; process 27.

### Ingredients
- [long-grain-rice] 300 g long-grain white rice, rinsed and drained
- [saffron] 0.15 g saffron threads (a small pinch)
- [vegetable-stock] 550 mL hot vegetable stock (check rice packaging for liquid needs)
- [fresh-tomatoes] 350 g small ripe tomatoes
- [onion] 1 small onion (100 g), finely chopped
- [garlic] 1 clove, finely chopped
- [olive-oil] 35 mL olive oil, divided
- [flat-leaf-parsley] 12 g parsley leaves
- [fine-salt] 2 g initially; adjust according to stock
- [black-pepper] To taste

### Method
1. Soak saffron in 60 mL of the measured hot stock for 5 minutes. If roasting tomatoes in the oven, heat to **200°C** conventional (180°C fan).
2. Toss the small tomatoes with 15 mL oil and roast on a tray 20 minutes until soft and slightly blistered, or blister in a skillet. These remain separate from the rice until serving.
3. Warm 20 mL oil in a lidded saucepan; soften chopped onion 4 minutes, add garlic for 30 seconds, then stir in drained rice for 1 minute.
4. Pour in all measured stock, including saffron infusion, and bring to a simmer. Cover and cook over low heat about 12–15 minutes (or according to rice type), until liquid is absorbed. Remove from heat and leave covered 5 minutes. Fluff with a fork; image 27 can show this final folding.
5. Spoon golden rice into a broad plate, top with roasted tomatoes, parsley and pepper. Adjust salt before serving.

**Allergens & checks:** Vegetable stock may contain celery, soy or other allergens; check label. Hot rice should be served promptly and leftovers chilled quickly rather than held warm at room temperature. **Pantry core:** rice, saffron, stock, tomatoes, onion, garlic, olive oil, parsley.

## R10 — Orange Olive Oil Cake
**Slug:** orange-olive-oil-cake · **Section:** Something Sweet · **Yield:** 8 slices (one 20 cm round cake) · **Prep:** ~15 min · **Bake:** ~35–45 min · **Cooling:** ~30 min · **Equipment:** 20 cm cake pan, parchment, bowls, whisk.
**Deck:** A soft golden crumb, fragrant with fresh orange and fruity olive oil.
**Imagery:** Finished 16; process 28.

### Ingredients
- [plain-flour] 190 g plain/all-purpose wheat flour
- [caster-sugar] 155 g caster sugar
- [baking-powder] 8 g baking powder
- [fine-salt] 2 g fine salt
- [eggs] 2 large eggs
- [olive-oil] 110 mL mild, fruity olive oil
- [orange] 1 large orange: 80 mL juice and finely grated zest
- [plain-yogurt] 100 g plain yogurt
- [vanilla-extract] 5 mL vanilla extract
- [orange] Optional: thin fresh orange zest curls for serving, taken from the same orange if enough

### Method
1. Heat oven to **175°C** conventional (155°C fan). Grease and line a 20 cm round cake pan. Do not invent extra baking mould shapes for photography.
2. In a bowl combine flour, baking powder and salt; whisk well.
3. In a second bowl whisk eggs and sugar until slightly thickened. Whisk in oil gradually, then yogurt, measured orange juice, zest and vanilla. Image 28 shows orange zest entering the mixing bowl.
4. Fold dry ingredients into wet just until no flour remains; do not overmix. Transfer to the prepared pan and level.
5. Bake approximately 35–45 minutes until the centre springs back gently and a skewer inserted into the centre emerges without wet batter. Cool in pan for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack and cool further before slicing.

**Allergens & checks:** Wheat/gluten, eggs, milk. Bake times vary by oven and pan; trial bake required before publication. **Pantry core:** flour, sugar, baking powder, eggs, olive oil, orange, yogurt, vanilla. **Editorial production note:** benchmark plausible flour/egg/oil ratios against reputable professional baking references, but this specific formula remains untested and must not be credited to another author.

## R11 — Honey-Roasted Pears
**Slug:** honey-roasted-pears · **Section:** Something Sweet · **Serves:** 4 · **Prep:** ~10 min · **Cook:** ~25–35 min · **Equipment:** baking dish, spoon, bowl.
**Deck:** Warm, glossy pears against cold yogurt and toasted walnuts.
**Imagery:** Finished 17; process 29.

### Ingredients
- [pears] 2 large ripe-but-firm pears (about 450 g), halved and cored
- [honey] 45 g honey
- [butter] 20 g unsalted butter
- [lemon] 15 mL lemon juice
- [plain-yogurt] 180 g thick plain yogurt, for serving
- [walnuts] 35 g walnuts, lightly toasted and roughly chopped
- [cinnamon] 1 g ground cinnamon
- [water] 30 mL water for the baking dish

### Method
1. Heat oven to **190°C** conventional (170°C fan). Halve and core pears, leaving the peel on.
2. Melt butter gently and mix with honey, lemon and cinnamon. Put pear halves cut-side up in a snug baking dish with 30 mL water.
3. Spoon glaze onto pears; reserve a little for basting. The image 29 process stage captures this, before the pears are roasted.
4. Roast about 25–35 minutes, basting once or twice, until tender when pierced and slightly amber at the edges. Add a spoonful of water if glaze begins to scorch.
5. Plate one pear half per person with a spoonful of cold yogurt, a little roasting syrup and chopped walnuts.

**Allergens & checks:** Milk and walnuts (tree nuts); honey is not suitable for infants under 12 months. Hot sugar syrup can burn. **Pantry core:** pears, honey, butter, lemon, yogurt, walnuts, cinnamon.

## R12 — Dark Chocolate Tahini Cookies
**Slug:** chocolate-tahini-cookies · **Section:** Something Sweet · **Yield:** 12 medium cookies · **Prep:** ~15 min · **Chill:** ~20 min · **Bake:** ~10–12 min · **Equipment:** oven, tray, bowls, whisk.
**Deck:** Deep cocoa, a soft centre and the roasted character of sesame.
**Imagery:** Finished 18; process 30.

### Ingredients
- [plain-flour] 150 g plain/all-purpose wheat flour
- [cocoa-powder] 30 g unsweetened cocoa powder
- [baking-powder] 4 g baking powder
- [fine-salt] 2 g fine salt
- [butter] 70 g unsalted butter, softened
- [tahini] 90 g well-stirred tahini
- [light-brown-sugar] 120 g light brown sugar
- [eggs] 1 large egg
- [vanilla-extract] 5 mL vanilla extract
- [sesame-seeds] 12 g sesame seeds for topping

### Method
1. Mix flour, cocoa powder, baking powder and salt in a bowl. Beat butter, tahini and brown sugar in another bowl until thoroughly combined.
2. Beat in egg and vanilla. Fold in dry ingredients just until a cohesive dough forms; chill around 20 minutes if too soft to shape.
3. Heat oven to **175°C** conventional (155°C fan); line a tray with parchment. Divide dough into 12 equal mounds, lightly flatten, add sesame. The raw evenly spaced pieces are image 30.
4. Bake roughly 10–12 minutes, until edges are set but centres remain slightly soft. Cookies will firm as they cool. Do not eat raw dough.
5. Cool on tray 10 minutes then transfer to a rack. Photograph irregular natural cracks and dark chocolate texture, not artificial brownie-like cakes.

**Allergens & checks:** Wheat/gluten, milk, egg, sesame. Raw flour and raw eggs should not be eaten. **Pantry core:** flour, cocoa, baking powder, butter, tahini, brown sugar, egg, vanilla, sesame.
