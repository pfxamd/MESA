An Ode to the Lemon.

The smallest way to change everything.

FROM MARKET TO TABLETHE EVERYDAY TABLE
VOLUME 01 / STORY 02BACK TO ALL STORIES ↗

Sometimes a dish does not need another hour. It needs a few drops of brightness.

Two kinds of brightness

A lemon brings both juice and zest. The juice tastes sharp and immediate; its peel carries fragrant oils that can make a dish feel vividly alive. They do different jobs, and knowing when to use each changes the result.

Zest first, before cutting the fruit, and keep the bitter white pith out of your finely grated peel.

A small finishing gesture

Try a squeeze of lemon at the very end of a bean dish, after tasting the salt. Add fine zest to cake batter, or let a few drops sharpen a dressing that tastes flat.

The goal is balance, not making everything taste of lemon.

Keep it simple

There is a particular pleasure in working with an ingredient that is so familiar. It takes very little to notice it again.

There is always something more to notice at the table.
